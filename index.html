05 :: HELP DESK & IEEE 754 REFERENCE FAQ

Having trouble? Click a question to read the answer.

How do I enter a decimal, hex, binary, or S/E/M value?

Decimal accepts values such as 345.26, -0, Infinity, and NaN. Hex is exactly eight digits (e.g. 3F9E147B). Binary is 32 bits. For S/E/M, enter 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits, and 23 mantissa bits. You can also prefix all S/E/M fields with 0x for hexadecimal input.

What is a normalized number?

For a normalized number, the exponent field is 1 through 254. Binary32 stores an implicit leading 1 in its significand.

\[x=(-1)^s\left(1+\frac{F}{2^{23}}\right)2^{E-127}\]
What is a denormalized (subnormal) number?

Exponent field zero and nonzero fraction: the significand starts with 0, and exponent is -126.

\[x=(-1)^s\left(\frac{F}{2^{23}}\right)2^{-126}\]
What are NaN, zero, and infinity?

Exponent 255 and nonzero fraction: NaN. Exponent 255 and zero fraction: signed infinity. Exponent and fraction both zero: positive or negative zero.

Why is 0.1 not exact?

Many decimals do not have finite binary representations. FloatLab parses decimal text as an exact rational number and rounds once to binary32 using round-to-nearest, ties-to-even. Addition, subtraction, and multiplication operate on integer significands and binary exponents, with guard/round/sticky information shown below the result. Directed rounding modes and exception flags are outside the project scope.
