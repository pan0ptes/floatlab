05 :: HELP DESK & IEEE 754 REFERENCE FAQ

Having trouble? Click a question to read the answer.

How do I enter a decimal, hex, binary, or S/E/M value?

Decimal accepts values such as 345.26, -0, Infinity, and NaN. Hex is exactly eight digits (e.g. 3F9E147B). Binary is 32 bits. For S/E/M, enter 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits, and 23 mantissa bits. You can also prefix all S/E/M fields with 0x for hexadecimal input.

What is a normalized number?

For a normalized number, the exponent field is 1 through 254. Binary32 stores an implicit leading 1 in its significand.

\[x=(-1)^s\left(1+\frac{F}{2^{23}}\right)2^{E-127}\]
What is a denormalized (subnormal) number?

Exponent field zero and nonzero fraction: the significand starts with 0, and exponent is -126.

\[x=(-1)^s\left(\frac{F}{2^{23}}\right)2^{-126}\]
What are NaN, zero, and infinity?

Exponent 255 and nonzero fraction: NaN. Exponent 255 and zero fraction: signed infinity. Exponent and fraction both zero: positive or negative zero.

How does FloatLab add two numbers? (worked example)

Take 13.25 + 2.5. In binary, 13.25 = 1.10101 × 23 and 2.5 = 1.01 × 21.

1. Align exponents: shift the smaller number right by the exponent difference (3 − 1 = 2), so 1.01 × 21 becomes 0.0101 × 23.
2. Add the significands: 1.10101 + 0.0101 = 1.11111, still × 23.
3. Normalize: the result already has the form 1.xxx. If the sum had reached 10.xxx, FloatLab would shift right by one and add 1 to the exponent; after a subtraction it shifts left until the leading bit is 1.
4. Round to nearest, ties to even: 1.11111 fits in 23 fraction bits, so nothing is dropped (G/R/S = 0/0/0) and the result is exactly 1.11111 × 23 = 15.75 = 0x417C0000.

Subtraction is the same with a signed significand. Multiplication multiplies the significands, adds the exponents, then normalizes and rounds the same way.

How do I read the G/R/S line?

After the exact result is normalized, only 23 fraction bits can be kept. The bits that fall off the end are summarized as G (guard, the first dropped bit), R (round, the second dropped bit) and S (sticky, 1 if any bit below those is 1). The rule is: G = 0 rounds down (truncate); G = 1 with R or S = 1 rounds up; G = 1 with R = S = 0 is an exact tie, so the result goes to whichever neighbor has an even last bit. The line also says exact (nothing was lost), incremented (rounded up) or retained (rounded down). Example: 16777216 + 1 gives G/R/S = 1/0/0, a tie, and the even neighbor 16777216 wins.

What does each class (normalized, denormalized, zero, NaN, infinity) mean?

Normalized: exponent field 1 to 254, with an implicit leading 1. Denormalized: exponent field 0 with a nonzero fraction; no implicit 1, exponent fixed at −126, so these are the tiny values below 2−126. Zero: exponent and fraction both 0; the sign bit still distinguishes +0 from −0. Infinity: exponent field 255 with fraction 0, which is what results when a value is too large to represent. NaN: exponent field 255 with a nonzero fraction, the result of undefined operations such as infinity − infinity or 0 × infinity.

Why is 0.1 not exact?

Many decimals do not have finite binary representations. FloatLab parses decimal text as an exact rational number and rounds once to binary32 using round-to-nearest, ties-to-even. Addition, subtraction, and multiplication operate on integer significands and binary exponents, with guard/round/sticky information shown below the result. Directed rounding modes and exception flags are outside the project scope.
